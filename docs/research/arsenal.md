# Operation Arsenal (Akcja pod Arsenałem), Warsaw, 26 March 1943: research notes

Research for the Minor Sabotage demo mission. Anything shown to players as history (the
end-of-mission note, captions, people cards) must come from this file or from
[`game/src/content/arsenal-history.json`](../../game/src/content/arsenal-history.json), which holds
the same facts in machine-readable form. Numbers in square brackets refer to the numbered
[Sources](#sources) at the end. Times are local (Warsaw was on Central European Time, UTC+1, on
26 March 1943; summer time began on 29 March [56]).

Compiled 2026-09-28. I did not read the printed books (Broniewski, Strzembosz, Domańska, Jastrzębski)
directly. Their content reaches this file through the Polish Wikipedia article, which cites them page
by page [1], and through the full text of Anna Borkiewicz-Celińska's chapter on the action [3]. Where
a later archival study corrects them, that is flagged ([4], on Hubert Lenk).

## What changed against the earlier brief

| earlier brief | corrected |
|---|---|
| 25 prisoners freed | **21 freed, Rudy among them** [1][7][8][6]. 25 was the figure in the Grey Ranks' own report of 29 March 1943, in the underground *Biuletyn Informacyjny* of 15 April 1943 and on the 1968 plaque. It was later corrected downwards [1]. One person riding in the van, Halina Siemieńska (Zośka's aunt), was killed by a stray bullet [1][8]. The sources do not say whether she is inside the 21. |
| 28 scouts | **28 by Broniewski's count (he commanded), 29 by Borkiewicz-Celińska's** [1][3]. The difference is Kazimierz Łodziński "Kapsiut", who stood in for a late arrival and handed over his pistol when that man came. Not counted either way: a second driver for the escape car, a three-person medical post and its five-person guard [1][3]. |
| The truck turned into Długa and ~10 m later into Nalewki, and the S-bend slowed it | That was the **plan and the regular route**. **On the day the truck did not turn into Nalewki.** Its driver heard Zośka's shots at a Polish policeman, abandoned the right turn and went straight on west along Długa. The bottles still hit it, and the burning truck rolled on slowly and stopped alongside or beyond the Arsenal [1][3]. The offset between the two street ends is **about 35–40 m centreline to centreline**, measured on the surviving street layout [50][43]. The "about 10 m" in the literature [1] does not fit the geometry (see Geography). |
| Germans: 4 killed, 9 wounded | Confirmed by Polish sources (Broniewski, Strzembosz and Domańska via [1]; [7]). The nine wounded include the truck driver and **two Polish "blue" policemen** [1]. The Museum of Warsaw entry gives 5 killed and 4 wounded [5]. |
| Hubert captured "and later killed" | Seized by a German bar owner in the gateway of **Długa 27** during the withdrawal [1][4]. Older accounts say he was beaten to death under interrogation, probably at Szucha two days later [1][15]. A 2018 archival study, citing the Pawiak Museum card index, says he was **shot on 7 May 1943 at Dzielna 21** [4][5]. |
| Stamps found in Rudy's flat | Found in the family's **cellar**, under a heap of coal: wooden stamps for the Fighting Poland anchor, with the whole archive of his sabotage work, leaflets, posters, torn German flags and fuse cord [1][3]. |
| Lola's news at about 13:45 | Confirmed. Helena Danielewicz "Lola" (Danielewiczowa in [3]) worked for the Prisoners' Aid Society (Patronat) and had access to Pawiak. She reported that Rudy had been taken to Szucha that morning. She had been expected at 12:00 [1][3]. |
| Rudy loaded at Szucha shortly before 17:00 | Confirmed. He was carried to the truck **on a stretcher**. Zygmunt Kaczyński "Wesoły" watched it happen and telephoned from inside the Gestapo building [1][3][37]. |

## 2. Order of battle

Numbers: 1 commander, **Atak** 14 (Zośka and four sections), **Ubezpieczenie** 14 (Giewont, three
sections and the car). That makes 29 [3]. Broniewski counts 28 because he leaves out Kapsiut [1]. Eleven
of them survived the war (Bartnicki, Broniewski, Chojko, Jastrzębski, Łodziński, Olech, Rodowicz, Saski,
Trzciński, Wolski, Zapadko) [1]. Their average age was about 21 [1]. Section membership follows
Borkiewicz-Celińska [3] and the English Wikipedia table [2], which agree. Items marked **(uncertain)**
are discussed under Conflicts.

**Arms** [3][1]: bottles of petrol; the Storm Groups' only two Sten guns, one each in Sten I and Sten II;
four grenades with the Granaty section; pistols. Some men carried two old Nagant police revolvers
each, because the revolvers were slow to reload. Orders were that documents need not be taken from
Germans, Polish police or armed civilians who were shot. A whistle started and ended the action, and
the retreat was to be on foot along Długa towards the Old Town [1].

### Group "Atak" (attack), commander Zośka

The four sections stood in successive lines on the pavement on the east side of Nalewki (the Pasaż
Simonsa side), facing the Arsenal, over "a dozen or so metres" [3][1]. Each was the next barrier if
the one before it failed [3].

| section | task | members (leader first) |
|---|---|---|
| (group command) | Planned the action and led the attack group | **Zośka**, Tadeusz Zawadzki (1921–1943): shot the intervening policeman, then his pistol jammed; took the safety off Słoń's Sten for him and led the decisive rush to the truck; rode with Rudy in the DKW. Cross of Valour. Killed 20 Aug 1943 at Sieczychy [1][3][12] |
| **Butelki** (bottles) | At the corner of Długa and Nalewki, opposite the Arsenal, nearest the crossroads. ## 3. Geography

### The street layout

The buildings around the crossroads were destroyed in 1944. The **street layout and the pre-war
paving survive**: the cobbled stub of Bielańska at Długa, and ul. Stare Nalewki, which keeps its
pre-war setts and old tram rails [1][42][45]. The distances below are measured on today's
OpenStreetMap geometry of those surviving streets [50]. They are good to a few metres, but they are
not a 1943 survey.

- **Długa** runs WSW–ENE through the crossroads. From the Arsenal it heads ENE (about 60°) towards
  Plac Krasińskich and the Old Town. West of the crossroads it runs WSW (about 240°) along the
  Arsenal's front towards the former ul. Przejazd, Tłomackie and the ghetto walls [50][1]. Orsza wrote
  that in 1943 the roadway sloped down that way, which is why the burning truck kept rolling [3].
- **Bielańska** comes in from the SSE and meets Długa from the south. For its last 60 m it heads
  NNW (about 330°). Further south it bends east-south-east towards **Plac Teatralny**, about 430 m in
  all [42][50]. Borkiewicz-Celińska mentions "the bend of Bielańska" [3].
- **Nalewki** leaves Długa to the NNW (about 332°), **parallel to Bielańska**, along the Arsenal's
  east wing, towards the ghetto [45][50]. On this stretch it was narrow [1].
- **The offset.** Nalewki's axis is about **35–40 m west (WSW) of Bielańska's axis** along Długa:
  38.5 m centreline to centreline in OSM [50]. The Długa infobox, 730 m and 770 m from Freta, gives
  40 m [43]. A truck therefore came north up Bielańska, turned **left** into Długa, ran about 35–40 m
  and turned **right** into Nalewki. That is the S-bend that made it slow down [1]. The "about 10 m"
  quoted in [1] (from Rossman, 1963) is far shorter than the axis offset. Treat it as the length of
  the short straight between two tight turns as the attackers felt it, not as a measurement. The
  measured value should drive the map.
- Borkiewicz-Celińska describes the double turn instead as Plac Teatralny into Bielańska and
  Bielańska into Długa [3]. Either way the crossroads was the last point before the van reached the
  ghetto, and the one farthest from Szucha, which gave the most time for the phone warning [1][3].
- **The regular route**, Szucha to Pawiak: al. Szucha, pl. Na Rozdrożu, Al. Ujazdowskie, pl. Trzech
  Krzyży, Nowy Świat, Krakowskie Przedmieście, Królewska (or Ossolińskich), pl. Piłsudskiego,
  Wierzbowa, **pl. Teatralny, Bielańska, Długa, Nalewki**, the ghetto gate, Nalewki, Nowolipki,
  Karmelicka (or Zamenhofa), Dzielna, Pawiak [1]. On Nalewki "a moment later came the barrier between
  the 'Aryan' side and the ghetto" [3]. The ghetto gate on Nalewki is marked by a memorial at the
  Świętojerska crossing [44], about 330–370 m up Nalewki from Długa [50].

### What stood on each corner

- **NW, north of Długa and west of Nalewki: the Arsenal**, Długa 52. A closed quadrangle of about
  **64 m along Długa by 82–83 m along Nalewki**, round an inner courtyard [40][50]. In 1935–38 it was
  converted from a prison into the **Municipal Archive of Warsaw** (Archiwum Miejskie). The conversion
  added an **arcaded pedestrian walkway, with a pavement, running the full length of the east wing
  along Nalewki**, and a sgraffito of Władysław IV and Józef Piłsudski on the Nalewki wall [40][1]. It
  was damaged in September 1939, and **in 1943 it still housed the Municipal Archive**, whose staff
  showed freed prisoners the way out [1][40]. Its arched main gate is on Długa: a 1939 photo filed on
  Commons as the Arsenal shows the gate with crossed timber beams in the passage [52]. The 1938 and
  1939 photos show the Długa front set back behind a strip of lawn with trees [53][52]. In today's
  geometry the Długa centreline runs about 20 m from the front [50]. Zośka's "we jump over barbed
  wire" during the rush to the truck [3] may refer to a fence on this strip; that is my guess, not a
  source. The post-war rebuilding (1948–50) did not restore the arcades [40]. On 26 March the
  attackers fired "from under the pillars" at the SE corner [3].
- **NE, north of Długa and east of Nalewki: Pasaż Simonsa**, Długa 50 / Nalewki 2. It was two
  buildings. The older one (1903) at the corner was bombed in September 1939, burnt out and partly
  pulled down, leaving only a fragment along Nalewki. The newer six-storey block (1904–06) stood
  further up Nalewki, beyond the small ul. Wyjazd [41]. Broniewski's plan labels the corner "Ruiny"
  (ruins) [3]. The attack sections stood on the pavement on this side [1][3].
- **SW, south of Długa and west of Bielańska, opposite the Arsenal**: tenements Długa 53, 51, 49 and
  47, and Długa 45 on the corner of Bielańska, as named on the 1939 panorama [51]. One tenement
  opposite the Arsenal had been destroyed in 1939. Freed prisoners escaped through its ruins to
  Tłomackie and then to Leszno, Rymarska and Bielańska [1]. The RGO welfare office from whose windows
  "Pytek" watched was on Bielańska [3].
- **SE, south of Długa and east of Bielańska**: blocks of houses [3]. A small restaurant "at the
  corner of Długa and Bielańska" held the relay telephone (Jur). Which corner is not stated [3][1]. A
  pastry shop on the Old Town stretch of Długa had its window shot out [3].
- **Further along Długa to the east**: the **Pałac pod Czterema Wiatrami**, Długa 38/40, on the north
  side. In 1943 it housed the main office of the German **Arbeitsamt** (labour office), from which
  Germans fired on the retreating scouts [48][1]. It is about 150–180 m from the crossroads [50].
  **Długa 27**, where Hubert was caught, is on the opposite side of Długa about 210–230 m from the
  crossroads; a plaque was unveiled there in 2024 [15][50]. **Plac Krasińskich** and Miodowa are
  about 400–410 m away [43][50].
- **On Bielańska**: the **Bank Polski**, Bielańska 10, on the east side about 250–280 m from Długa,
  where Kadłubek stood and where Marciniak and Kiwerski were during the action [47][50][1]. On the
  other side of Bielańska was the mouth of **Tłomackie**, where Kuba stood [1]. How far up or down
  the street from the bank is not recorded.

### Trams

Tram rails ran along Bielańska between Plac Teatralny and Nalewki (the section opened in December
1881) and along Nalewki [49]. A stretch of the old track survives in the cobbles of Stare Nalewki
[45]. Its southern end curves in from the east, consistent with rails running from Bielańska through
Długa into Nalewki [50]. **I could not confirm from a reliable source which lines, if any, used this
junction in March 1943.** A user-drawn schematic of 1941 lines shows a tram route from Plac Teatralny
towards Plac Krasińskich outside the ghetto, with the Nalewki tracks inside the ghetto marked out of
use [54]. It is not authoritative. No account of the action mentions a tram.

### Where the truck was stopped and attacked

1. The truck came up Bielańska and turned left into Długa. At the Nalewki corner the driver, alarmed
   by Zośka's shots, pulled left instead of right and carried on west along Długa [1][3].
2. The Butelki section at the corner (Pasaż Simonsa side) threw as it passed. The first bottle missed
   and fell on the road. The second hit, slid off the windscreen and the bonnet onto the pavement. The
   third, Bolec's, broke the windscreen, and the cab caught fire [1] (Orsza remembered four bottles
   landing on the bonnet [3]). The driver slumped over the wheel. The two Gestapo men beside him jumped
   out trying to beat out their burning uniforms. The engine died, but the truck rolled on slowly
   along the sloping Długa, following the curve of the driver's last swerve [1][3].
3. It stopped "beyond the Arsenal" (Broniewski; Zośka) or "opposite the entrance to the building"
   (Jastrzębski) [1]. A photo caption in [1] (the Arsenal seen from the south-west) says it stopped
   near that spot. For the map:
   **on Długa, alongside the Arsenal's front, some 30–60 m west of the Nalewki corner.** This is my
   reading of those descriptions and the building's size, not a sourced figure.
4. Sten I and Sten II crossed Nalewki westwards and fired from under the arcades at the Arsenal's SE
   corner, together with Butelki. Buzdygan was hit while crossing by the wounded policeman lying in
   the road [1][3]. The two guards in the back fired calmly and accurately for several minutes. The
   attackers stayed pinned under the arcades, and their weapons kept failing [3].
5. Słoń's Sten jammed. Zośka took the safety off for him, Słoń leaned out and fired bursts, and
   they ran for the truck. Zośka: "We jump over barbed wire." One guard jumped out wounded, tried to
   hide on the far side of the truck and was put out of action. The other was found sitting dead on
   the right-hand side; Zośka had fired at him while running up. Kołczan opened the tailgate [3][1].

### Where the groups withdrew

- **As planned: on foot, east along Długa towards the Old Town** [1][3], past the Stare Miasto
  section, the pastry shop and the Arbeitsamt, then into the crowds on **Miodowa** and in the Old
  Town, or across Plac Krasińskich [1][3]. Orsza and Giewont left last, took a horse cab at Kapucyńska
  to Plac Napoleona, and saw Anoda strolling along Krakowskie Przedmieście [3].
- **The DKW** reversed from the Długa–Bielańska crossroads to the truck, loaded Rudy and Buzdygan,
  and drove east along Długa (it passed Katoda), through the Old Town towards the city centre and
  Mokotów, to **Ursynowska 46** [1][3].
- **Alek's car** was a civilian car stopped on Długa. It was blocked by a Wehrmacht truck at the exit
  onto Plac Krasińskich; after Alek's grenade it turned back towards Miodowa and went on to Żoliborz
  [1][3].
- **The Opel with Maria Schiffers** went west along Leszno and was fired on outside the courts building
  (Leszno 53/55) [1].
- **Freed prisoners** went east along Długa towards the Old Town, or into the Arsenal grounds, where
  the archive staff pointed the way. From there they crossed through the ruined house opposite to
  Tłomackie [1].

### Light at 17:30

Computed, not sourced. On 26 March 1943 Warsaw was on CET [56]. At 17:30 the sun was about 3° above
the horizon, due west (azimuth about 269°), low along the axis of Długa beyond the Arsenal. Sunset
was about 17:58 and civil dusk ended about 18:33 (standard solar formula). This matches "it is
starting to get grey" [3].

## 4. The truck and the escort

- **The vehicle**: a **Renault truck covered with a tarpaulin, registration 72076** [1][3][5]. The
  prisoners sat on **benches across the load bed, backs to the direction of travel** [1]. About 20
  prisoners per transport [1]. It ran Pawiak–Szucha at about 08:00 and back at about 17:00, through
  a double gate onto ul. Dzielna [3].
- **The escort**: **four Gestapo men armed with handguns.** **Two sat in the cab beside the driver.**
  **Two sat in the back at the two ends of the bench nearest the tailgate** [1][3]. The driver was
  SS-Unterscharführer Alfred Habicht, who was wounded [1].
- **How it was stopped**: petrol bottles at the cab. Bolec's third bottle set it alight, the driver
  was hit, and the two cab guards bailed out burning [1][3]. The stens were meant as the second and
  third barriers. Sten I was to fire almost perpendicular at the cab. Sten II was to run into
  Nalewki and fire at 45° [1]. In the event the truck was already burning and rolling when the stens
  came in, and they fought the two rear guards from the Arsenal arcades [3].
- **How the prisoners got out**: after Zośka's rush, **Kołczan opened the tailgate** and the
  prisoners poured out over each other [3][1]. Rudy came last, crawling on all fours over the
  benches, and was carried to the DKW. The DKW had reversed from the crossroads to near the burning
  truck, and Buzdygan was already in it [1][10].
- **Afterwards** the Germans changed the transport: a different type of vehicle, a stronger escort,
  and varied routes [1].

## History note draft

Checked sentence by sentence against the sources above. The numbers used are 28 scouts (Broniewski's
count), 21 freed (the corrected figure) and 4 killed and 9 wounded on the German side (the Polish
literature).

### English (≈255 words)

On 26 March 1943, near the Arsenal in Warsaw, where Bielańska, Długa and Nalewki streets meet, 28
scouts of the Grey Ranks' Storm Groups attacked a Gestapo prison truck. The action, code-named
"Meksyk II", was planned by Tadeusz Zawadzki "Zośka" and commanded by Stanisław Broniewski "Orsza".
Its aim was to free Jan Bytnar "Rudy", arrested on 23 March and tortured by the Gestapo, as he was
driven from their headquarters on Szucha Avenue back to Pawiak prison.

At about 17:30 bottles of petrol set the truck on fire. After a fight with its escort the scouts
opened it, and twenty-one prisoners were freed, Rudy among them. The German side lost four killed
and nine wounded.

Rudy died on 30 March 1943 of the injuries inflicted during his interrogation. That same day Maciej
Aleksy Dawidowski "Alek", shot in the stomach during the withdrawal, died in hospital. Tadeusz
Krzyżewicz "Buzdygan" died of his wounds on 2 April. Hubert Lenk "Hubert" was caught during the
withdrawal and later killed by the Germans.

On 27 March 1943, in reprisal, the Germans shot 140 prisoners of Pawiak, Poles and Jews, in the
prison courtyard.

Zośka was killed on 20 August 1943 in an attack on a German border-police post at Sieczychy. The
Home Army battalion formed from the Storm Groups was named "Zośka" after him. Orsza led the Grey
Ranks from May 1943 to October 1944, fought in the Warsaw Uprising, survived the war and died in
2000. He later wrote a full account of the action, *Pod Arsenałem* (1957).

## Conflicts between sources

1. **Prisoners freed: 21 or 25.** 25 appears in Marciniak's report of 29 March 1943, the *Biuletyn
   Informacyjny* of 15 April 1943, the 1968 plaque, the body of the English Wikipedia article and the
   Museum of Warsaw entry [1][2][5]. 21 is the later correction (Broniewski 1993, Strzembosz 1983,
   *Encyklopedia Warszawy* 1994 via [1]), and it is what the Polish Wikipedia, dzieje.pl, IPN, Polska
   Zbrojna and the English infobox use [1][7][6][8][2]. Whether Halina Siemieńska, killed in the van,
   is inside the 21 is unclear.
2. **Participants: 28 or 29.** Broniewski gives 28, Borkiewicz-Celińska 29 (adding Łodziński) [1][3].
   The IPN release says "kilkunastu" (a dozen or so), which fits neither [6].
3. **German losses: 4 killed and 9 wounded** (Broniewski, Strzembosz and Domańska via [1]; [7]; [2])
   **or 5 killed and 4 wounded** (Museum of Warsaw [5]). IPN gives four killed [6].
4. **Hubert's death**: beaten to death under interrogation about 28 March 1943, probably at Szucha
   (Broniewski, Borkiewicz-Celińska, Strzembosz, Polska Zbrojna [1][3][8][15]), or **shot on 7 May 1943
   at Dzielna 21** (Pawiak card index and Domańska 1978, via Borkowski [4]; the Museum of Warsaw
   [5]). Borkowski explicitly calls the first version an error. Bartoszewski's "died in a camp" is
   also wrong per [4]. Hubert's company is given as "Południe"/"Sad" [15] or "Centrum" [4].
5. **Where the truck stopped**: "beyond the Arsenal" (Broniewski; Zośka) or "opposite the entrance"
   (Jastrzębski) [1].
6. **The bottles**: three thrown, the third decisive (Jastrzębski via [1]), or "four bottles land on
   the bonnet" (Orsza via [3]).
7. **The S-bend distance**: "about 10 m" [1] against about 38 m axis to axis on the surviving layout
   [50][43]. And which two turns made the S: Bielańska–Długa–Nalewki [1] or Plac
   Teatralny–Bielańska–Długa [3].
8. **The order of events in "Meksyk I"**: stand-down first and then the phone call (Zośka's
   account, Borkiewicz-Celińska [3]), or the reverse (Broniewski's book, per [3]).
9. **Heniek's arrest**: the night of 17/18, 18, or the night of 18/19 March 1943 [3][1][39].
10. **Rudy's interrogations**: at Szucha on Tuesday, Thursday and Friday (Broniewski), or not
    transported on the two days after the first (Strzembosz) [3].
11. **Who received Jur's message**: Zośka [3] or Orsza [1]. And from when Alek was acting company
    commander: 24 March [11] or the Thursday (25 March) briefing [3].
12. **Kapsiut's place**: Borkiewicz-Celińska counts him in a five-man Getto section as Felek's
    stand-in [3]. The Polish Wikipedia lists him next to the Stare Miasto section and, from his 2003
    interview, says he covered towards Plac Krasińskich [1]. Both agree that he handed his pistol to
    Felek and went off towards Plac Krasińskich [1][3].
13. **Tytus's first name**: "Jerzy Trzciński" [1] or "Tytus Trzciński" [2][3][4].
14. **Tadeusz Szajnoch's death**: 1 May 1962 in France (the article text, which cites the Warsaw
    Rising Museum biogram) against "died 1945" (the same article's category). He is also absent from
    Broniewski's list of eleven war survivors [23][1]. I did not read the biogram itself.
15. **The Zdanowicz raid**: three people arrested on 27 March [1], or four in the night of 26/27
    March, all of whom survived Auschwitz [4].
16. **Sommer's execution**: 18 July 1943 at Józefów station [1][4], against Jastrzębski's 12 July,
    which [4] calls wrong. "Osjan" [1] and "Oddział Specjalny Jan" [4] are the same Kedyw unit, so the
    two do not conflict on who carried it out.
17. **Women among the prisoners**: "6 women" [2], against 9 women among 19 identified [1].
18. **The interrogator's first name**: Herbert Schulz (Polish sources) against "Hubert Schulz" (English
    Wikipedia) [1][2].
19. **Last surviving participant**: a 2008 headline called Bartnicki the last [26], but Wolski (d.
    April 2011) and Łodziński (d. July 2011) outlived him [27][31].
20. **The 1958 plaque** gave the date as 25 March [1].
21. **Tadeusz Chojko**: the PDF of [3] prints "Tadeusz Bójko". That is taken here as a scanning
    error; the Wikipedia biography is Chojko [18].
